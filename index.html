About

Joan deBien-Trammell is a strategic leadership researcher and the founder of Organizational Meaning Science, a research discipline examining meaning as a systemic force that shapes perception, behavior, identity, and coordination within individuals, teams, and organizations.

Her work translates organizational complexity into durable frameworks, decision architectures, and participatory assessment methodologies grounded in rigorous research and applied across enterprise, nonprofit, and emerging organizational contexts.

"Most strategy fails because it's disconnected from how organizations actually make sense of the world. My work helps organizations see patterns, interpret signals, and act with clarity."

Drawing on decades of operational leadership experience—including roles at L'Oréal, Gallup, Pierre Fabre, and Sandoz—and advanced doctoral research in strategic leadership, she designs meaning-based frameworks that help organizations make coherent decisions in complex, regulated, and high-stakes environments.

Research & Intellectual Property

Organizational Meaning Science

Established foundational research discipline examining how meaning functions as a systemic force within organizations. Developed core constructs including the Meaning Loop, response state dynamics, interpretive divergence, and meaning safety as measurable phenomena.

ITES™ Instrument

Provisional patent for Interpretation Through Experience Scale—a proprietary methodology measuring interpretive states and meaning dynamics within organizational systems without extracting content or evaluating performance.

Meaning Architecture

Theoretical and applied frameworks for designing organizational systems that protect meaning stability, support interpretive pacing, and enable ethical measurement of meaning climates over time.
